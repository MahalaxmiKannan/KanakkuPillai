# Index fund and SIP

**What it is:** An index fund is a mutual fund that holds the companies in a market index, such as the Nifty 50, instead of a manager picking stocks. A SIP (systematic investment plan) is a way to put in a fixed amount every month.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Risk** | High. The value moves with the market and can fall sharply, including for years. There is no guaranteed return. |
| **Lock-in** | None for a regular index fund. |
| **Liquidity** | Good; usually money arrives within a few working days of selling. |
| **Return** | Not fixed. Past returns don't predict future ones. |
| **Tax** | Gains are taxed when you sell, at rates that depend on how long you held. Check the current rules. |

**Good for:** Money you won't need for many years and can leave alone during falls.
**Watch out for:** Selling in a panic, fees (the "expense ratio"), and anyone promising fixed or "guaranteed" returns on market products.

Mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI. The Simulator page shows a range of possible outcomes, not a prediction.

*Rates and tax rules change, so no figures are quoted here. Last reviewed: November 2026. Check current ones at [SEBI Investor](https://investor.sebi.gov.in) and [AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com).*
